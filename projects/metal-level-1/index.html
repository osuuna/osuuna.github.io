07 / Cal Poly Pomona workshop

METAL Level 1 Manufacturing Workshop

At Cal Poly Pomona’s METAL Level 1 workshop, I made aluminum parts using green sand and lost foam casting, then practiced lathe work, CNC milling, and steel heat treatment. Taking designs from CAD through casting and finishing helped me understand manufacturing processes and the importance of designing for manufacturability.

01 /

Green sand casting and machining

We poured molten aluminum into a green sand mold to create the truck body, turned its wheels on a lathe, and CNC milled the casting on a Haas Mini Mill to remove excess material and surface imperfections. Working through casting and machining showed me how the initial shape and finishing operations work together to produce the completed part.

02 /

Lost foam casting

We modeled the horse in CAD and made foam patterns from the design. The patterns were packed in sand and replaced by molten aluminum during pouring to form the horse casting. I also used Fusion 360 for modeling and FLOW-3D CAST to look for potential shrinkage defects.

03 /

Designing for manufacturability

Making both aluminum castings helped me connect design decisions to the manufacturing process. I learned to consider how a shape will be cast, which surfaces will need machining, and how the part can be held and reached by the tools. The experience reinforced the importance of planning fabrication and finishing while developing the design.

04 /

Heat treatment trade-offs

We used time–temperature–transformation diagrams to reason about how thermal history changes steel microstructure. We then secured heat-treated coupons in a vise to compare their behavior. The exercise made the trade-off tangible: hardness helps resist indentation and wear, while ductility and toughness matter when a part must deform or absorb energy before fracturing.
