04 / Air quality instrumentation

Tethered Weather Balloon

I led a team building a tethered balloon payload to profile how air quality changes with height above Mt. San Antonio College in Walnut, California. The project combined mechanical packaging, embedded electronics, and ground operations.

  • Raspberry Pi

01 /

Payload enclosure

We chose ASA for outdoor UV and temperature exposure. The enclosure made room for the sensor breadboard, with a louvered panel and a dedicated opening for the LoRa antenna. We estimated balloon lift from payload mass and used the expected loads to evaluate the eye bolt and select a tether within our length and budget limits. A manual winch was planned for recovery.

02 /

Sensing and logging

I drew the KiCad schematic for the Pico, Pi Zero 2 W, radio, camera, and sensor connections. The breadboard integrated the BME280, MiCS-6814, MQ-131, and PMS5003 for atmospheric and air quality measurements. Python and MicroPython handled sensor acquisition; the Pi logged readings to a CSV on its SD card. LoRa was intended to send a second copy to the ground if the payload could not be recovered.

03 /

Field outcome

The balloon burst during inflation after contact with a hot metal bar, so the payload never flew and we collected no altitude profile. A loose camera ribbon also prevented planned footage. For a repeat attempt, I would isolate the balloon from hot surfaces, secure the camera connector, and verify recording and telemetry on flight power before inflation.
